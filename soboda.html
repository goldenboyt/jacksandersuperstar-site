02 / Field notes

Field notes
from the Taiga.

First encounter of the Soboda was in 1960's in Alaska. Found to be only in caves due to it's barebones nature. Evidence of this creature can be found by its almost human-like footprints. Only 40 were ever to be recorded in human history.

Strains of the Soboda's hair can be found around the Taiga. Often to be mistaken by the American Black Bear as it has the same texture and pungence. When put through a DNA test in 2005 it was discovered that it was the ancestors of the Black Bear.

To lure prey, the soboda can imitate sounds from other animals and humans very well. It has a very strange and odd behavior pattern when faced with danger, The Soboda stands up on its two hind feet and screams at the predator to encourage them to be frightened by its almost human-like nature.

In order to survive the harsh climate of the Taiga, the Soboda often switches sexes to procreate. In the off chance that a Soboda is killed, it will bleed smegma. The Soboda often lives a short life at around a month. In order to reproduce, the Soboda will lay eggs around trees, ponds or caves.

03 / The discoverer

Dr. Rasha

The man who discovered the impossible

Born in New York in 1940, Dr. Rasha has been the leader of the NYU Discovery Center for Medicine and Care. He's 9'7 and has 7 kids and 3 wives. He's always wanted to become something more. Upon the discovery of the Soboda, he changed the world for the way better. He's off the grid, got tats on his ribs he unfortunately has passed away in Madrid. His brain is preserved and is believed to be used to power the NYU University.
